Humayun's Tomb

Overview

Humayun's Tomb in Delhi is the tomb of the second Mughal emperor, Humayun.

  • It is regarded as the first garden-tomb on the Indian subcontinent.

History

Humayun died in 1556. His wife Bega Begum commissioned the tomb, and it was built between 1565 and 1572.

  • The architect was Mirak Mirza Ghiyas, who came from Persia.

Architecture

The tomb stands on a raised platform in a charbagh, a Persian-style garden divided into four parts by water channels. It uses red sandstone with white marble details and a double dome.

  • Its design influenced later Mughal buildings, including the Taj Mahal.

Conservation

A major restoration of the tomb and its gardens by the Aga Khan Trust for Culture was completed in 2013.

UNESCO World Heritage Site

Humayun's Tomb was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1993.
