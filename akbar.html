Akbar

Overview

Akbar (1542-1605), also known as Akbar the Great, was the third Mughal emperor. He ruled from 1556 to 1605.

  • He was born at Umarkot in Sindh on 15 October 1542.

Early Reign

Akbar became emperor at the age of 13 after the death of his father, Humayun. Bairam Khan acted as his regent, and Akbar's army won the Second Battle of Panipat in 1556.

  • He later expanded the empire across much of northern and central India.

Administration

He built a system of governance based on mansabdari ranks and a land revenue system developed with ministers such as Todar Mal. He also ended the jizya tax on non-Muslims in 1564.

Religion and Culture

Akbar held discussions among scholars of different faiths at the Ibadat Khana in Fatehpur Sikri. In 1582 he promoted Din-i Ilahi, a spiritual idea that few people followed.

  • His court historian Abu'l-Fazl wrote the Akbarnama.

Legacy

Akbar died in 1605 at Agra and is buried at Sikandra. His reign is remembered for strong administration and a policy of tolerance.
