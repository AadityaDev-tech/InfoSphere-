Red Fort

Overview

The Red Fort (Lal Qila) is a historic fort in Delhi, built of red sandstone. It was the main residence of the Mughal emperors.

  • Its walls run for about 2.4 km around the complex.

History

Shah Jahan ordered its construction in 1638 when he moved his capital from Agra to Delhi. It was completed in 1648 and stood at the heart of the new city of Shahjahanabad.

  • After the revolt of 1857 the British took control and used the fort as a military garrison.

Architecture

The fort has an irregular octagonal plan. Its main entrance is the Lahori Gate, and inside are halls such as the Diwan-i-Am and the Diwan-i-Khas.

  • The design, traditionally attributed to Ustad Ahmad Lahori, blends Mughal, Persian and Indian styles.

Cultural Significance

Every year on Independence Day (15 August) the Prime Minister of India hoists the national flag at the Red Fort and addresses the nation.

UNESCO World Heritage Site

The Red Fort Complex was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2007.
