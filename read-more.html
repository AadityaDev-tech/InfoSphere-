Overview

The Taj Mahal a white-marble mausoleum located in Agra, Uttar Pradesh, India. It was commissioned by the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal.

  • The monument forms part of a larger complex containing gardens, a mosque, a guest house and other structures.

History

Construction of the Taj Mahal began during the reign of Shah Jahan in the 17th century. Mumtaz Mahal died in 1631, and Shah Jahan commissioned the mausoleum in her memory.

  • The principal mausoleum was completed in the 1640s, while work on parts of the surrounding complex continued afterward.

Architecture

The Taj Mahal is primarily constructed from white marble. Its main structure has a large central dome surrounded by four minarets

  • The complex contains decorative elements including carved marble, stone inlay and calligraphy. Its design combines features associated with Mughal, Persian and Indian architectural traditions.

Location

The Taj Mahal is located on the southern bank of the Yamuna River in Agra, Uttar Pradesh.

Gardens

The monument is surrounded by a large formal garden. The garden contains pathways and water channels arranged around the central axis of the complex.

Cultural Significance

The Taj Mahal is an important monument of Mughal-era architecture and is one of the most recognized historical buildings in India.

  • It is also an important tourist destination and has become widely associated with India's cultural heritage.

UNESCO World Heritage Site

The Taj Mahal was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1983. UNESCO describes it as an important example of Indo-Islamic architecture.
