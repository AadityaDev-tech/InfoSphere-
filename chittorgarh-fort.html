Chittorgarh Fort

Overview

Chittorgarh Fort is one of the largest forts in India, set on a hill in Rajasthan. It was the capital of the Mewar kingdom for centuries.

  • The hill rises about 180 m above the plain, and the fort covers around 280 hectares.

History

The fort was besieged three times: by Alauddin Khalji in 1303, by Bahadur Shah of Gujarat in 1535, and by Akbar in 1568.

  • After 1568 Chittorgarh was no longer the Mewar capital. Udaipur, founded by Udai Singh II in 1559, took its place.

Monuments

Famous structures include the Vijay Stambh (Tower of Victory), built by Rana Kumbha in the 15th century, and the Kirti Stambh, a Jain tower from the 12th century. The fort also holds palaces, temples and water tanks.

  • The Vijay Stambh is about 37 m tall.

Cultural Significance

The fort is tied to stories of Rajput courage, including the legend of Rani Padmini and the jauhar carried out during the sieges.

  • Some of these accounts come from later literary traditions rather than records written at the time.

UNESCO World Heritage Site

Chittorgarh Fort is part of the Hill Forts of Rajasthan, inscribed in 2013 together with five other forts.
